This page says what that means in practice. It is meant to keep people from being confused about who runs a server they are signing in to, not to get in the way of anyone using or improving the software.

## What you can do without asking

- Run Orbit for yourself, your team or your company, and say it is "powered by Orbit" or "built on the open-source Orbit project".
- Write about Orbit, review it, teach it, and link to the repository.
- Describe a fork accurately, for example "a fork of Orbit with extra templates".
- Show the unmodified name and logo to say that your product works with, or is compatible with, Orbit.
- Distribute unmodified builds of the project under its own name, with a link to the source.
- Put your own details in the legal pages (see below). That is expected.

## What needs written permission

- Using "Orbit" or "Aionyx", or a name that could be mistaken for either, as the name of a hosted service, company, product or domain that you offer to other people. This includes names such as "Orbit Cloud" or "Aionyx Orbit".
- Using the logo or brand artwork as the identity of your own service, or in a way that suggests Aionyx runs, endorses or supports your service.
- Presenting a modified version as the official Orbit, or as the Aionyx hosted service.

If you offer Orbit to other people, please give your service its own name and logo, and make it clear on the pages people sign in on that you, and not Aionyx, run it.

## Running a public copy

If other people sign in to your copy, you are its operator. Set `ORBIT_OPERATOR_NAME`, `ORBIT_CONTACT_EMAIL` and `ORBIT_DATA_LOCATION` so the privacy page, terms and data policy name you. The hosted-service terms and privacy policy written by Aionyx cover only the service Aionyx runs. See [docs/self-hosting.md](docs/self-hosting.md#you-are-the-operator).
